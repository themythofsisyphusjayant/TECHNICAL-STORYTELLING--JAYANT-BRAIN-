# Story Template

Copy this file into a new folder under `stories/` — `stories/NN-short-slug/STORY.md` — and fill it in. Keep the five-part shape. Keep my voice: plain, direct, short sentences, honest about what I failed at and what is still unknown. No hype, no generic AI phrasing.

---

# Story NN — <Title>

**Project:** [repo name](repo URL) · **Status as of <date>.** <One line: where the project stands right now.>

## 1. The problem

What was actually wrong, in plain words. Not the pitch — the real difficulty. What made this worth building at all, and what would have happened if nobody built it.

## 2. Design decisions

What I chose, and why, with the trade-offs stated. Name the alternatives I considered and rejected, if the record shows them. A decision without its trade-off is marketing.

## 3. Failed attempts

What didn't work, with specifics: what I tried, what happened, what the failure proved. This is the heart of the story — give it the most space. Include the failures that were embarrassing, the ones caught by a check, and the ones that cost real time. If a failure changed a design, say so here and again in section 5.

## 4. Lessons

What the failures taught, stated as rules I now follow. Each lesson should trace to a specific failure in section 3. No generic wisdom — if it could have been written before the project started, it doesn't belong here.

## 5. What changed the plan

How the goal, the design, or the schedule moved because of what happened. Dated entries work well: what changed, when, and why. Include what's still open and what's blocked — and on whose word it's blocked.

---

## Sources

List every source the story rests on: the project repo (README, code, results), the local work tree, memory notes, the paper. Link them. If a claim has no source, it doesn't go in my story — it goes in a gap marker.

## Honesty rules

- **Every number, date, decision and failure needs a source.** If I can't point at it, I don't write it.
- **Mark gaps, don't fill them.** Where the record ends, I write **[I still need to write this part — …]** and move on. Those gaps are mine to fill in.
- **Report losses.** A baseline that beat me, a claim that got narrowed, a deadline I missed — all of it stays in.
- **No filler.** If a section has nothing real to say, I say that in one line. A short honest story beats a padded one.
- **Date-stamp fast-moving projects.** If the project changes weekly, I put "Status as of <date>" at the top and write what is verified as of that date. I don't chase the live count.
